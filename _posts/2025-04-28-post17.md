<br/>
As most of my friends and family now know, I'm on a mission to visit all 50 U.S. states — no layovers, just a sufficient experience of each state's culture and food. I started this trip at 32 and am now at 36, thanks to some solid adventures with A in the Deep South Highlands, with drives filled with banter, tunes, and the occasional Andy Serkis-narrated audiobook.
<br/>
<br/>
Leaving SJC at 9:30am on 04/22, I had a relatively smooth flight to BNA (Nashville), with all the turbulence crunched into the final 15 minutes. A had driven up from Atlanta and stocked the car with fruit, nuts, and granola bars. After checking into Best Western Brentwood to drop our bags, we explored the Gulch and capped the night with a lighted view of the local Parthenon. Creamy mac-n-cheese with hot sauce at Peg Leg Porker BBQ was followed by A's foray into focaccia at St. Vino's Focacceria — the best he'd had to date. Since carb-loading was a signature of this trip, we offset it by renting e-bikes from a BCycle stand (a Philly-based e-bike sharing company now in nearly 30 cities, founded a year before Uber with 1000 bikes at the 2008 DNC and RNC). Of the two bridges to pedal across, we wisely chose the Seigenthaler Bridge for picturesque photos of the Korean Veteran Memorial Bridge against a Cumberland River sunset. The electric assist could barely handle our weight on the slopes, so we got a decent workout traversing Wasioto Park, the Ghost Ballet sculpture, and the Schermerhorn Symphony Center. After a first-time Jeni's ice cream stop and a sumptuous dinner at bartaco (adobo piña + pollo verde tacos, fried plantains, esquites), we photographed the lit-up Parthenon and called it a night.
<br/>
<br/>
Having packed so much into seven hours after a 3:50pm arrival, A and I valiantly slept in and missed the free continental breakfast Wednesday. After eggs, bacon, and cheese grits at Biscuit Love, we headed for NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Alabama, with my debut appearance at Buc-ee's. About a mile out, a federal officer at an inbound security gate cooked us, shamelessly telling us to turn around and go to the "play-play center where the kids do their field trips" — with such contempt that, honestly, I respect the steadfastness of the energy. At the U.S. Space and Rocket Center we were greeted by the epic A-12 Oxcart (one of 13 total), flown as part of the CIA's Operation Blackshield in 1967; fifty years later, in 2017, it was fully restored (paint removal, silicone sealant, scuff sanding, priming, gloss coats). I loved that the museum wasn't only weapons and defense, but captured the history and lore of many nations' space travel and the space-inspired sci-fi of the late 20th century. We traced every Mars rover model, satellite-powered lightning and tornado detection, a hypership simulation ride, and a full mechanical breakdown of the Saturn V, which grew in parallel with Projects Mercury and Gemini. The lunch that followed was poor — food took forever, and the servers were uncharacteristically standoffish with the only two bearded brown guys in the restaurant — which encouraged us to bolt for Mississippi before dusk. En route to the quaint town of Corinth (by way of Iuka), we faced a ludicrous downpour so heavy the high beams reflected back at the windshield, but we made it through for tasty rolled ice cream and three games of checkers at a local eatery. Mississippi's food service beat Alabama's tenfold (sample size: two), and after many a windy nighttime road and pizza at a sports bar watching the Warriors get cooked in Game 2, we returned to Brentwood.
<br/>
<br/>
Every morning A and I would realize how much we'd eaten the day before and semi-seriously discuss a three-day fast. And like clockwork, every day at noon, we'd push forward like fearless stallions, galloping toward our next four or five meals. Thursday was the zenith, in variety and quantity. Waking full, we left late morning for Louisville, and 40 minutes in hit what was indubitably the worst freeway jam of my life. I-65N was at a near-standstill for almost 30 miles; what should've been a 2h17m drive to the Lincoln Birthplace Memorial took nearly five hours (plus an hour to the time-zone change). A's a hero for getting us through. Stuck for hours, I went for a walk outside the car and we nearly finished Black Mirror's seventh season before finally crossing the site of the accident, wreckage fully cleared. Then we got stuck behind a nimble 5mph tractor for another 50 minutes. Reaching the memorial at 3:40pm, we grabbed pamphlets, jogged the whole trail and stairs, and left in 15 minutes to speed-run the Muhammad Ali Center and the Bourbon Trail History Museum before dinner at Wild Swann — fittingly an Old Fashioned in its town of origin, with wagyu bacon sliders. Gaining an hour on the drive back, we reached the hotel around 9pm and, on a wave of inspiration, decided to end the night with Indian food. Honest Vegetarian Indian Restaurant in Antioch (20 minutes away) came in clutch; we bolted to arrive at 9:42pm before the 10pm close. Seated at 9:45, the owners asked us to eat fast as the kitchen closed. A housed a gobi manchurian and a chole bhature in 6 minutes 30 seconds, some kind of world record. I took a few minutes more, and as we left, the owners looked into our eyes with a mixture of gratitude and fear. We passed out cold after warm showers (I needed four digestive miles on the treadmill first).
<br/>
<br/>
Overall a 10/10 trip and the first of many! Subpar dining in AL but an excellent rocket center; subpar weather in MS but great dining vibes. And an amazing time at AT + AL's wedding in South San Jose that Sunday, days after returning.
