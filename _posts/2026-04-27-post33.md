<br/>
Some people visit the Midwest for the food, the lakes, or the Mall of America. Our crowning achievement was managing to reach a random rock without a flat tire, but we procured all three of the aforementioned aspects anyway :)
<br/>
<br/>
A and I reached Omaha around 11pm on Friday (4/24) and drove a lap of downtown at midnight, with the skyline lit up and the I-480/680/29 signs glowing effusively on the windshield. Not deviating from South Asian stereotypes, we rented a gray Honda Civic and managed to photograph a mysterious crust on the rear quarter panel of the car for insurance purposes, prior to even leaving the garage.
<br/>
<br/>
Saturday started with a neon sign reading "It Was All a Dream" (not the most ideal omen for a road trip), then pizza at 9am from Orsi's Italian Bakery & Pizzeria, which has been open since 1919. A personal pan with garlic bread before noon is a great way to start a vacation, and the framed Foxtrot strip by the register, about a kid paying for a pizza entirely in pennies, sold us further on the place. The Italian uncle up front was a no-nonsense fellow, but he also was extremely reliable with our tight schedule.
<br/>
<br/>
Next was the Lewis & Clark Interpretive Center in Sioux City, where we spent the better part of an hour between the York exhibit, the shelves of Corps of Discovery books, and a placard on "Punishment for Slackers." The expedition kept a military routine for over two years. We were on day two and already needed coffee.
<br/>
<br/>
Coffee came in Sioux Falls from Scooter's (an Omaha-born chain, ngl a proud find), followed by a Cybertruck lurking in the rainy parking garage and a lunch of spinach-tomato-feta omelet under two TVs playing fireplace videos. We walked it off past the Giant Lever ("Can you lift a car?", an SDSU engineering exhibit from 2004) and a triceratops squarely guarding the science center.
<br/>
<br/>
The afternoon was mostly prairie: passed exits for Akron and Alcester/Wakonda, hit a visitor center that was CLOSED FOR THE SEASON, and stopped at a hilltop rest area where the only other car was a white Buick that looked like it had been stolen. Around 5:30pm, after putting our poor Civic through a tortuous and torturous journey on a country mile of rocky dirt roads, we managed to reach the infamous tripoint where Minnesota, North Dakota, and South Dakota meet. Touted as a truly magnificent landmark, it comprises of a red granite marker next to a drainage ditch and a boulder pile, plus an orange box holding a guest book, where a previous visitor had noted how hard the wind was blowing; that part was accurate. We took the requisite selfies with the N.D. stone, signed in to the guest book, and stood in a field being deeply pleased with ourselves and equally anxious about dragging the poor Civic through the path that we came.
<br/>
<br/>
That night in Minneapolis we ate at Moto-i, a sake izakaya with bright red neon sign out front. The bao buns and crispy brussels sprouts were tasty, but the mushroom ramen was the best ramen I've tasted in the United States.
<br/>
<br/>
On Sunday, after a hotel breakfast next to a lobby-sized fake waterfall, we spent three and a half hours at the Mall of America, and we'd defend every minute. Nickelodeon Universe under the skylights, the LEGO store (a Pan Am DC-3 set and an alarming number of Hogwarts castles), iconic Forrest Gump merch ("Stupid is as stupid does"), the FlyOver America simulator and wall map, and a Sugar Factory milkshake menu that can best be described as a cry for help. Lunch at Rainforest Cafe was fried cheese curds (Minnesota, after all), pasta, and flatbread. We also passed the "Do Something" sculpture for Tom Burnett of Flight 93, who was from Bloomington. True legend, and even cooler to later learn that he had a long and successful career working at a medical devicees company based in the Bay Area.
<br/>
<br/>
We closed out the trip at the bucolic harbor in Wayzata on Lake Minnetonka, under a gray sky and bare trees just starting to leaf, staring at lake houses out of the budget range. 4 states down!
