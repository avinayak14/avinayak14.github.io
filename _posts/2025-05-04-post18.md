<br/>
RKD and the boys have been amazing friends for the past 10 years, and it was such a pleasure to celebrate our ~Penguin~ of a lad on his special occasion in the warm city of Daytona Beach. SK and I shared a hotel room at the local Hilton to save cost, and most of the other groomslads had procured similar rooms either through Super or the standard way.
<br/>
<br/>
Flying in through Denver, I was supposed to land in Orlando around 7:45pm but got delayed; S reached a bit after me, so we took the cost-efficient shuttle straight to the hotel. Most folks were asleep by then, but we ran into SD and RP for a mini bool sesh with some late-night energy. The next day would be busy, and since I was singing Friday evening, I was actively conserving my voice.
<br/>
<br/>
We awoke early around 7am and got ready for the Haldi. Everyone trickled in slowly, and S and I met the rest of the gang at the main hotel room. After smearing RD in yellow, we carried him like a pack of cavemen yelping down to the ocean and dunked him — a fantastic experience through and through. K was spared the dunking but included in all yellow-smearing activities.
<br/>
<br/>
After resting in the afternoon, we got ready for the evening sangeet, where I sang and where RD had asked me to join the dance-off with a few others. My rendition of Aaj Kal Zindagi (from Wake Up Sid) landed as an optimistic, positive message to the couple without being too sappy, and D+S brought epic, maximal energy to the dance-off. After the garba — which RD kept dancing through even as he shed a gallon of sweat — we had a bit more dancing, accompanied by tasty chaat and Indo-Chinese delicacies.
<br/>
<br/>
The next day was the wedding, so the groomsmen suited up early. The baraat kicked off with nearly every possible remix of Bollywood and modern English music, during which a very special someone had his foot run over by the golf cart leading RD in the procession. But he's a hero — he still crushed his responsibilities the rest of the day and crushed the dance as well.
<br/>
<br/>
After pictures outside the main fountain, we proceeded to the actual venue, and it was HOT. Groups of us cooled off in the seating area, and after the Saptapati, all the groomsmen threw flowers on the couple — a beautiful moment. RD, the fantastic negotiator, bargained his shoes back on the condition that all present gentlemen (SK, RP, and I) take the bridesmaids to lunch; as the gentlemen we are, we obliged. After an... interesting... lunch at "Dick's Last Resort" (where staff are instructed to be rude), we rested a few hours before preparing to go absolutely all-out at the reception. I ran into SD, WI, and Doshi in the lobby and went up to RD's room to help S practice the dance before our evening sesh.
<br/>
<br/>
The groomsmen's entrance at the reception stands above many other spectacular moments as my favorite. To "Make Some Noise for the Desi Boyz," we walked in stoic, then broke into our boolerous dance, starting the night with unbridled energy. Great speeches from the family, a well-executed friends' choreo, and hours of dancing to everything from Bollywood to rave followed, then an afterparty where we kept the boolerousness going. At one point D would shout "can I get a ____", and we'd all repeat the "____" with nfg.
<br/>
<br/>
My flight was the next morning, and I shared an Uber to the airport with two of the bridesmaids. In the wise words of D, "we'll never forget the weekend that we made a real-life Bollywood movie together for our boi."
