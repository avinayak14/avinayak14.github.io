<br/>
Attending F1 races live is incongruous with my tax bracket, but General Admission $80/weekend IndyCar isn't. Plus it was my cousin S's bachelor party in Tampa.
<br/>
<br/>
Reaching around 12:15am Saturday Feb 28, T and I met at the airport to pick up a rental car and drive back to the Airbnb to catch the tail end of Blood on the Clocktower (BoTC, similar to Mafia). Nearly everyone was in bed by 1:45am to catch six or seven hours of sleep.
<br/>
<br/>
Most of us started slowly, and around 9:45am we headed to Pipo's for a semi-early brunch — solid Cuban sandwiches and roast pork with picadillo, on brand for a bachelor party's first full meal to have 17 guys around a table of 10. Great portions regardless, and then we headed to the Firestone Grand Prix Quals in St. Petersburg. After 45 minutes of trying to park (halfway through, T and I dropped a couple of guys off so they could catch more), we found a garage spot about a 20-minute walk away. Walking over, T and I grabbed refreshments and arrived as the trucks raced before the main quals — ridiculously unnerving to watch pick-ups gunning it at over 100 mph (top speeds apparently reach 160–170, reduced on the St. Pete course because of all the turns).
<br/>
<br/>
IndyCar is underrated. I'd implore budget-conscious F1 fans to check out a U.S. race — they run in several cities. A day on track isn't just racing; there's entertainment, great food and drink, and live music. Ours was in the heart of the city, prime for an afternoon bar stop. After a full day of racing, we cantered over to Big C's Chopped BBQ for incredible Southern smoked meats and sides (I got the chicken sandwich with mac'n'cheese and candied yams). Just as we were about to order, the man himself — "Big C" — walked in, introduced himself gregariously, and took our orders before making them. Not gonna lie: dude was big. His charisma showed exactly how he'd built the business, especially since his margins are strategically thin given local demand — a full chopped chicken tray for $5 in a town the size of Tampa is no joke without McDonald's brand and distribution.
<br/>
<br/>
After picking up our food, around 85% of the guys (myself included) were ready to Uber back to the parking lots. But the man of the weekend somehow convinced nearly 15 reluctant individuals to take the local bus instead, orders in hand. The next thing we knew, we were on local transit — chaotic, bumpy, but authentic, and a roguer yet far better way to see the city at night than a fleet of Ubers. After a couple more rounds of BoTC, we called it a night. I left early Sunday morning while some went to the day's races and left later.
<br/>
<br/>
An incredible weekend to celebrate a special dude!
