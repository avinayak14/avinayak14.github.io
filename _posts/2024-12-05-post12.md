<br/>
I hadn't been to Hawaii in 10 years, so when a trip with Bay Area friends came up, I hopped on it.
<br/>
<br/>
After a sumptuous Thanksgiving, 11/29 kicked things off. Reaching Honolulu in the afternoon, we hiked Diamond Head, a volcanic tuff cone on Oahu, with the full crew. Climbing about 500 feet from the crater floor, we explored multiple bunkers and viewed a lighthouse built in the last two years of WWI. Fortification began in 1908 as part of the U.S. Army Coastal Artillery defense system, with the fire control station added in the years before WWI and maintenance continuing through WWII and statehood in 1959. Descending, we were esurient for food and landed at a Hawaiian-Italian fusion spot called Heavenly Island Lifestyle, which lived up to its name. Four of us had citrus tomato spaghetti with Italian sausage and burrata, plus a lychee spritz of vodka, liqueur, bitter, and prosecco. We spent five minutes debating "lychee," where my pronunciation was thoroughly overruled — perhaps correctly. After shaved ice, we immediately regretted skipping the matcha soft serve.
<br/>
<br/>
The next morning, after acai bowls, we split up (kayaking vs. a jungle expedition and golf at Kualoa Ranch). After S and I took some stupendous swings on the driving range, we toured Kualoa Ranch — a rugged open-air Jeep ride into the Hakipu'u rainforest, learning Hawaiian history for the first 45 minutes and about movie sets for the last. Our guide explained how American missionary physician Gerritt Judd became an advisor to King Kamehameha III and eventually bought over 600 acres of Kualoa land, now owned by his descendants (the Morgan family). "Kualoa" means "long back," the mountain range said to resemble the back of a mo'o (lizard), with the island of Mokoli'i as its tail. We also glimpsed sets from Triple Frontier, Kong Skull Island, Jumanji, and Jurassic World. After a surfeit of 'Ulu chips, we met the others at Lanikai Brewing Company, sharing a jalapeño-pineapple pizza with fresh pulled pork (pineapple, Ananas comosus, goes extremely well with pizza). After a sunset and hula performance at Kuhio Beach, we grabbed Korean BBQ and said goodnight — and goodbye to M and H, leaving the next morning.
<br/>
<br/>
Sunday made up for Saturday's lack of water time with an all-day beach day at Hanauma Bay. K, S, D, and I picked up umbrellas and chairs via a subsidized hotel program to avoid being assiduously roasted, then headed to the beach. Snorkeling in the open ocean for the first time, I saw so many fish near the reef — Moorish Idols, Pacific Trumpetfish (yellow and gray), Raccoon/Teardrop Butterflyfish, Sailfin/Yellow Tangs, and more. After Hanauma, a large group of us went cliff jumping at China Walls (Koko Kai Mini Beach Park). I took a 15–20ft plunge and got slightly cut on the mossy rocks coming back up, but nothing serious. After tempura, we hit "MOSA!", a crepe and ice cream place with four free Super Smash Bros. Ultimate controllers (which we examined but didn't use), and called it a night.
<br/>
<br/>
Working Monday through Wednesday, I brought my laptop along Monday, when A/J/K/D/S and I spent the day in the quaint North Shore town of Hale'iwa, exploring art galleries and shaved ice. After Laniakea Beach, we stopped in Pupukea for decadent Paniolo ("cowboy") burgers (bbq sauce, house sauce, onion rings, bacon) and ube acai bowls, then caught the sunset at Sunset Beach Park and snorkeled a bit more. With most folks leaving Tuesday, I caught S and B in the lobby on their way out; after poke in Waikiki with S/K/D, they headed to the airport too. The rest were leaving around midnight, so we got poke round two at Maguro Brothers in the evening, then saw Moana 2 at the Ward Center in Kaka'ako. A few Maguro dishes had uni (sea urchin), and A taught me it was used for centuries in coastal East and South Asia as a butter substitute — far more economical for cooking, especially noodles and rice. Moana 2 was very good, though not Moana 1 (an eight-year gap builds expectations). Wednesday was work by day and travel by night.
<br/>
<br/>
A phenomenal trip! It'll be jarring not to see poke and musubi every 100 feet outdoors.
