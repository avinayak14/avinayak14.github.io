<br/>
I'm thankful for a wonderful trip to Dubai for P&A's wedding, despite my aunt's passing on the last night I was there.
<br/>
<br/>
After the workday on Wednesday, S kindly dropped me at the airport from my new place in RWC; my flight was around 7pm. Though I've typically slept well on flights, I've recently noticed it's SIGNIFICANTLY easier with an eye mask. One was used. I slept nearly 10 hours of the 12-hour flight to Istanbul and re-read parts of Death's End the rest of the way. After a two-hour layover, I flew on to Dubai, landing around 3am Friday (11/28).
<br/>
<br/>
In Dubai I went straight to our hotel, the Dukes in Palm Jumeirah — and OH MY GOODNESS, it was fancy. P&A kindly sponsored our rooms. My roommate E was only arriving Friday night, so at 5am everyone was asleep and I was wide awake after all that plane sleep. I FaceTimed AM (whose Friendsgiving I'd missed for the trip), said hi to the Waterloo homies, and saw an epic spread of dishes (SB's bacon-spinach stuffed chicken looked like the grand prize). Then I FaceTimed K&A, road-tripping in Humboldt County, to wish them a happy Thanksgiving (they brought back fantastic Hum Yum sea-salt caramels).
<br/>
<br/>
With hours to kill before lunch, I worked on two personal projects — quantitative metric analyses of the best NFL and cricket players right now, and what makes each prolific. Then Doshansari and I got lunch at Revo Cafe at the NH Collection (including an impossible-to-finish Impossible Burger) before the Dubai Mall. If the Westfield near Santana Row is paying Dwyane Wade, the Dubai Mall is paying LeBron; the density of luxury retail was astronomical. They wanted perfumes, so we stopped at Arabian Oud, a popular Saudi fragrance chain with over a thousand locations globally, scoring a buy-one-get-one-free deal. Then a tea-tasting shop where staff chanted "Habibi, welcome to Dubai" and kept handing us free samples (a questionable business model), and we ended at HAFIZ MUSTAFA 1864 for plain and Dubai-chocolate baklava.
<br/>
<br/>
Running tardy, the three of us bolted to the hotel, ran into D&M, sprint-changed, and headed to the Burj Al Arab for the Welcome Social. A wonderful event by the water, catching up with all our Princeton friends and A's friends before the wedding — high-value since the couple is always busy on the day. Some went clubbing; being a boomer, I headed back with the early-sleeper lads, but not before leading a 15-person dance practice and grabbing dinner with Doshansari and Waka Flocka Flame at Khyber, the Indian restaurant on our hotel's terrace. Fantastic chicken jalfrezi, ngl.
<br/>
<br/>
The next morning the lads EW, RD, and I had a pregame brunch (at the "Great British Restaurant") and a beach day before the Haldi/Mehndi, swimming laps in the sea, then walked the one minute to the venue. A delicious menu of chaat, sandwiches, bhel, dahi bhalla, and desserts; after a four-hour nap we headed to the evening Sangeet for the Friends' dance — a medley of Tauba Tauba, Tum Hi Ho Bandhu, and Sapphire — and everyone crushed it, followed by a festive mix of Hindi and English songs. The night was just getting started: I was somehow(???) convinced to join the crew for a Calvin Harris concert at Ushuaia (no idea how I mustered the energy, but I did).
<br/>
<br/>
The wedding was the next day at Palm Jumeirah, cars everywhere. It was super fun riding in the limo, and A came in for his baraat in a Ferrari — baller. Then a delicious spread and the loveliest wedding right on the beach. Unfortunately, about an hour after we returned to the hotel, I got a text about Akka and headed back to the U.S.
<br/>
<br/>
All in all, a wonderful vacation and reunion with all my college friends to celebrate two very special people. Akka would have wanted me to enjoy the trip and make memories, and I'm so glad I did — and that I got to see her and give her hugs before I left on the 26th.
