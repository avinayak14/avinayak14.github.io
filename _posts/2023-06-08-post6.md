<br/>
Legend has it Princeton Reunions, the annual alumni event, necessitates the second-largest beer shipment in the U.S. behind the Indy 500. I hadn't been to campus in four years, so I combined the trip with visiting K & S in Cleveland.
<br/>
<br/>
After a busy workday on Friday 05/26, I flew SFO to Philadelphia with a backpack and a carry-on. United's quality was a non-increasing function over the trip, but on Friday I still held them in high spirits. I was keen to sleep after boarding, but the excitement of reunions kept me awake nearly two hours. No all-nighter, thankfully, but I only clocked three hours of sleep.
<br/>
<br/>
In Philly that morning, I had a sumptuous all-you-can-eat breakfast at a hotel with family and friends in town for a wedding. En route from the airport to downtown, there are visibly more billboards for lawyers in Philadelphia than in all of California. Pennsylvania's flat income tax is the lowest of the flat-rate states (3.07%), so top attorneys have that going for them compared to California's levels.
<br/>
<br/>
Reaching campus, I was overcome with nostalgia. It's hard to appreciate college amid midterms, finals, recruiting, problem sets, essays, and over-scoped group projects. But walking through the Fitz-Randolph gates again, I felt deep gratitude for being the first in my family to attend and graduate from a U.S. university. Tuition and board weren't easy, but I can safely say the historic buildings, sculptures, and spacious classrooms mean nothing without the classmates who filled their chambers. I'd entered expecting Princeton to be unaccommodating for someone with my background; I leave humbled to have met students from all walks of life, and to have learned more from them than I ever could sitting in a lecture hall.
<br/>
<br/>
After sushi with a few friends (including J, a year older and from high school), I went to the P-Rade to catch up with my class. Then a sizable group hit Tower (my old eating club) for burgers, and about 25 of us ended up at one of my favorite Nassau St spots, Thai Village. The Pad Thai (adjustable spice) is phenomenal, and we spent a couple hours being way too boisterous. Huge props to the staff for telling us to be as loud as we wanted, so long as it was in the spirit of alumni enthusiasm. We made several people chug several times (except me — I don't drink much), drawing surprised looks from nearby elderly couples.
<br/>
<br/>
After dinner, a group of us headed to Powers Field for the 10:15pm fireworks, and stayed over an hour. The fireworks and band were spectacular. Then we pre-gamed (a sacred tradition) and headed to the tents, grouped by five-year intervals, where all the parties happen. SK and I hadn't bought wristbands, so massive props to RKD/AA/DM for scouting and sneaking us in via wristband reuse. We danced and vibed until around 2am, then hit Frist for a classic late meal. Fries taste way better at 2am. Around 3am we crashed in our friends' hotel room (thanks AJ, RD, VN, and JT).
<br/>
<br/>
Sunday morning, five of us (sadly missing SD, who had to work) had a wholesome boyos brunch at Tacoria. Unfiltered banter, career ideas, STEM/math/finance/business, sports, and life. Then I gathered my luggage and flew EWR to CLE.
<br/>
<br/>
In Cleveland, KK picked me up and we headed to their spacious place with a comfortable guest bedroom. The evening weather was immaculate, so the three of us walked around lower Shaker Lake. We (S) tried climbing trees and doing cartwheels, but I realized I'm far less comfortable with 10–12ft heights without a harness than I was a decade ago (bouldering excepted — padded landing).
<br/>
<br/>
The next day S worked at the hospital, but K and I had Memorial Day off. We tried the Cleveland Museum of Art and the Museum of Natural History, only to find both closed after arriving twice. The natural history museum — the one I most wanted to see — had its first doors unlocked but the second locked, leaving expectations unmet. We ended up at the Cleveland Cultural Gardens, a collection of 35 public gardens in Rockefeller Park, one per country. The Ukrainian garden holds a statue of Lesya Ukrainka, dedicated in 1961 and designed by Mykhailo Chereshniovsky.
<br/>
<br/>
Later that afternoon and evening, we went to the Cleveland Botanical Gardens — intriguing plants, butterflies, and a fair share of stray rabbits and squirrels. Then K and I hit Johnny Mango, a Caribbean bar, for non-alcoholic mango and beet drinks, cornbread, and nachos. Crispy-edge cornbread with hot sauce and/or chipotle sauce is insurmountable as a food.
<br/>
<br/>
Tuesday to Friday, K, S, and I all worked during the day but made time for fun in the mornings and evenings. Wednesday morning K and I went to the Washington Reservation driving range (golf is something I want to do more), and in the evening we played tennis nearby. Thursday night the three of us had a nice dinner (the beet risotto was delicious) at Poppy in Buckeye-Shaker, with dessert at Mitchell's Ice Cream in Van Aken (the wild berry crumble and salted caramel were amazing).
<br/>
<br/>
Before work on Friday 06/02, K and I played nine holes at Washington Reservation over two hours, where I intentionally didn't keep score. United hit a stratospheric low that evening: my flight was supposed to depart at 7:47pm but got delayed five times with intractable comms, finally leaving at 9:06am the next day. I rebooked for Saturday evening without a surcharge and relaxed at K+S's place before departing back to SFO — no further unsolicited changes, thankfully. On the flight I watched two solid movies: The Independent and A Man Called Otto. All in all, a wonderful eight days on the east coast!
