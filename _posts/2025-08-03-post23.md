<br/>
If life hands you a week to celebrate three nuptials of three special couples in three locations spanning opposite sides of the globe — plus a day in Doha — don't pass it up.
<br/>
<br/>
It's common knowledge my north star is 30 countries by 30 and all 50 U.S. states by 30, so this trip made good headway on the country count. Leaving Saturday to reach Colombo early Monday morning, the best route was Qatar Airways (my first time). Landing around 3am Monday (IST locally, though the plane thankfully wasn't on IST) after Gran Turismo and BTTF on the way, I Ubered to my lodging, grabbed morning coffee and brunch, and worked from the hotel and nearby café Grind until a little after noon.
<br/>
<br/>
After work, a field day: A and Ke picked me up and we met KD at the Lovi fitting boutique, where the manager explained the history of the Nilame suit (KD's wedding attire) — created and donned by the Kandyan aristocracy, muted by British tailoring in the Colonial era, and revived after independence in 1948 — and how its patterns evolved. After arranging the fit pickup, we walked the One Galle Face beachfront to the main mall, spending nearly an hour hunting sunglasses for me :P Ten escalator rides and thousands of steps later, I found an affordable pair for ~$14 (on par with my CVS standards).
<br/>
<br/>
Trying lots of local tea was a big goal, and we delivered: Basilur for chai and torched salmon sushi, the Shangri-La main hall's architecture, an ayurvedic spa shop in the Dutch Hospital Shopping Precinct, and peach tea and chamomile at Dilmah's t-lounge, capped by dinner and cocktails at Virticle with incredible night views. It's not easy staying awake 3am to 10pm, but having the besties by your side helps.
<br/>
<br/>
Tuesday and Wednesday were work in the mornings, sightseeing in the afternoons, and work or social plans in the evenings. Tuesday: work 8am–1pm, then five solid hours exploring — Viharamahadevi Park, the Colombo National Museum, the Sri Lankan Cricket Museum, Gangaramaya Park, and more. The national museum was a treat, covering irrigation and manufacturing techniques across the Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa, Dambadeniya, Gampola, Kotte, and Sitawaka periods; the evolution of local coinage (punch-marked coins to kahapanas to 1950s Central Bank issuance); and the influence of Theravada Buddhism, Hindu Shaivism, Sunni Islam, and Catholicism on architecture from 1200s stupas to Galle's Moorish minarets and Kataragama's Dravidian gopurams. Wednesday, during and after work, I visited the Gallery Cafe, the Lotus Tower, and the Sima Malaka temple, then a fun group dinner with A+K, A's Masi, and family on the Virticle rooftop before flying out Thursday morning.
<br/>
<br/>
In Doha I had the day to kill between flights, so I met two friends: one for an early dinner, one for a late one. At a calm 115°F outside, the best move was a cool café to finish work, so I went to the Desert Rose Cafe in the Qatar National Museum for a hummus platter (including pomegranate) and much-needed iced coffee. After the museum, dinner #1 with J at SMAT — chicken kebab masala penne, zaatar samboussa, and roast lamb shank, over tech, business, college nostalgia, and music. After boxing me out of the bill Asian-uncle style (which happened again later with N and again with S in Chicago), J drove me around the whole city rather than have me walk in 100+ degrees, giving a historical account of key businesses, the Pearl, and Qatar's energy and manufacturing economy over the last two decades. At Souq Waqif, N and I met at Parisa for lamb and delicious fereni, catching up on ethical AI and education — well-timed, right after the GA of ChatGPT Study Mode (which my friend AM actually worked on). On the way back to the airport, it was settled: Qatar has my heart, but I'm electing to return only when highs are sub-95°F.
<br/>
<br/>
I luckily caught 12.5 hours of sleep on the 15-hour Doha-to-DC flight, so reaching at 8:30am Friday there were no energy limits. After calling K for his 29th, I headed to A Aunty's friend's house to shower and change for the morning Hindu wedding at Chinmaya Mission. The ceremony was completely founder-moded by V Uncle, and it was great to see all the cousins and bachelor-party guys in festive Indian attire. After the ceremony and a traditional Konkani lunch on banana leaves, I took a few calls on the temple Wi-Fi while others headed back to prepare for the reception. With all my luggage on me, I freshened up and changed quickly in the restroom.
<br/>
<br/>
The evening event was at the Salamander in central DC. After welcomes and appetizers, S&P gave a 500/500 speech to open the night; after a few more speeches, I was invited to give a short speech and sing "Can You Feel the Love Tonight," and both went quite well. Plenty of appreciative comments after, and a great rest of the sangeet. The next morning T dropped me at the airport, and no delays getting to Chicago. After "Brave New World" on the plane, I showered and changed at the Hyatt before A+N's wedding — and oh my goodness, what an event to cap the week. A wonderful reunion of high school friends (some I see often, some not in a while), with wholesome speeches and performances all around. I've known A for 20 years, one of the smartest, most genuine people from high school, and it was great to meet his Penn friends too. The next morning, a nice bagel brunch at R&A and boba at Stringria with my Pton boi SK and his girlfriend before the airport. Chicago is truly underrated.
<br/>
<br/>
For every single group meal on this trip, I was boxed out of paying. Folks have serious skills — and more importantly, they're kind-hearted, incredible hosts. A 10/10 trip that somehow had ZERO flight delays. This week was wild, and it'll be calming to rest from wedding travel for... four days... until NS's wedding... back in NoVA :)
