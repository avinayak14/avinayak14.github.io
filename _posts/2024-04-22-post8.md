<br/>
LA is one of my favorite weekend getaways from the Bay Area. I made a short two-day trip to see my cousin S (recently engaged!) and his fiancée M, and to celebrate the birthday of my close college friend J, who also went to high school with me. My friend S was at Coachella, so I'll catch her in May when she visits SF.
<br/>
<br/>
Since my flight was early Friday morning, I stayed Thursday night with friends (K+A) in the Peninsula, much closer to SFO. Friday at 4am I rolled out of bed, brushed, and slept my way to the airport in an Uber. On the flight I was thoroughly entertained by three guys behind me bashing past United flights in Hindi. The flight was just over an hour. At LAX I spent almost 15 minutes outside trying to figure out why the Uber app was forcing Uber Black/Luxury over UberX at Arrivals. Turns out there's a system called LAX-it, launched October 29, 2019; my prior LA trips had been driving, bus, or family pickup, so I'd never needed it. LAX is mid-construction on a people mover, a multi-modal transit station, a parking structure, and terminal upgrades, and LAX-it is a stopgap for travelers exiting the airport until real intra-airport transit arrives. UberX picks up at the LAX-it zone, which I reached by shuttle. The system has noble goals but needs work to meet the goal of minimizing passenger friction. LA's weather makes it more tenable, but east coast pickup areas risk declining traveler sentiment from similar models. And the loss of curbside convenience is hardest on elderly travelers, families with young kids, and people hauling heavy luggage — the exact groups forced toward Uber Black when the walk to LAX-it is too much.
<br/>
<br/>
I reached the office around 8:30am and grabbed a breakfast burrito from the market café. After a second burrito for lunch, I grabbed coffee with college friend A in the Arts District at Kodo, then strolled through Little Tokyo before finishing a few more hours of work. Around 6pm, with M & S an hour out, I looked for a gym to freshen up. The onsite gym was members-only, but I struck up a fun conversation in Spanish with K, a Honduran woman on the cleaning staff who'd studied programming and math and was working shifts to save for further education.
<br/>
<br/>
Once M and S arrived around 7pm, I gave them a quick office tour; they astutely noted the layout corroborates every Silicon Valley meme. Being huge Southeast Asian food fans, we went to Luv2eat Thai Bistro near West Hollywood — incredible panang curry, eggplant green curry, spicy basil fried rice, and drunken noodles. After groceries, we watched the F1 Chinese Grand Prix Sprint Quals at home.
<br/>
<br/>
The next morning, M made homemade crepes with berries, bananas, whipped cream, and peanut butter while we watched F1 Q1/Q2/Q3 Quals. Verstappen is an animal. Around 10:30am they drove me to J's birthday brunch at Pijja Palace, which I cannot overstate my love for — practically 15% of the reason I visited. Okra tater tots, dosa onion rings, mint chutney pizza, malai rigatoni, cookies & cardamom soft-serve. On the way to pick up the cake, the Accord flashed a yellow battery light and the infotainment display started flashing between HD Color and pitch black. One light before the freeway, the car died — alternator failure, no electrical power left, only mechanical (doors, trunk). We pulled over just in time, and J's dad secured a tow truck via AAA. A challenging situation, but grateful it happened in broad daylight, fully off the freeway, with J's dad helping a ton.
<br/>
<br/>
We cancelled the cake-cutting at J's but still picked up the cake (they added two inches after hearing our predicament!) and cut it at home — the apricot preserves inside were delicious. With 90% left, I had to catch my evening flight, so J and I said goodbye. Back at SFO, friends A+K picked me up and we hit a rooftop Jazz Night in SF; they'd brought my suit so I could change out of hoodie and shorts.
<br/>
<br/>
Overall, a short but fun trip!
