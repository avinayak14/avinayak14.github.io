<br/>
One of our dearest friends from college, the lad SD, was getting married in Jaipur in March, and there was no way the boys and I weren't going to pull up.
<br/>
<br/>
First, a few words about S. One of the most accomplished people I know, he makes every conversation he joins 10x more fun; his ability to laugh and make those around him laugh is infectious. Through highs and lows he's a very solid friend, and N happens to be correspondingly dope.
<br/>
<br/>
Travel was complicated by the Iran situation, but I was fortunate to score a reasonably priced deal flying west through Singapore (Star Alliance 4 life). AJ kindly let a group of us stay with him in Mumbai before flying to Jaipur together, and it was such a great time. After reaching Mumbai on the morning of the 24th, I headed straight to A's place to drop my belongings, and we spent a fun day at the mall. Lunch at Cream Centre, chai at Bombay Sweet Shop, then back to the house to relax and practice our dance, plus massages that were FIRE. A had booked Sahib Room & Kipling Bar at the St. Regis, which had incredible lamb chops among the other dishes.
<br/>
<br/>
The next morning we woke around 5:15am with chai and headed to the airport for our morning flight to Jaipur. IndiGo was smooth, and we reached in time to catch transport to the Anantara hotel, where the wedding was held; there's nothing like stepping out of a taxi to be greeted by several boolmaxxing dhol players with curled handlebar staches. After settling into our rooms, we headed straight to the Carnival event — more sights, sounds, and colors than any of us could have predicted. S and N rode in on an elephant, grand as ever, and joined a decibel duel, where the GROOM'S SIDE WON at 133 dB. The bois delivered.
<br/>
<br/>
After checking in and squeezing in a late-afternoon dance practice with the instructor in the ping pong room, we were set for the evening Sangeet, with every type of dish imaginable — Indian, Italian, Mexican fusion, the whole nine yards. We booled through our stage performance of Lungi Dance, Dhurandhar's Title Track, and Badtameez Dil, followed by an incredible set from Swaraag, an Indian fusion band based in Rajasthan. We afterpartied till around 2 or 2:30am.
<br/>
<br/>
The next day brought the Haldi in the late morning, but first an incredible Anantara breakfast — my favorite was the Moonglet station, a plant-based omelette of soaked yellow mung daal. The haldi was full of energy, with speeches by W/A/R, and after the religious celebrations came a water-gun armada and a tug-of-war the groom's side ONCE AGAIN PULLED THROUGH. After that cardio, the jhalmuri and chole kulche were on point, enabling a two-hour power nap before the evening.
<br/>
<br/>
As many weddings as I've attended, the baraat never gets old. After we partied S to the main stage, we enjoyed a wholesome ceremony against the fully lit Anantara hotel, followed by a delicious dinner of (literally) all cuisines. After getting the afterparty going past 3:30am (S+N only showed around 2:30 or 3), I somehow woke at 7am for breakfast and a workout. AA, SP, and I explored Jaipur — Hawa Mahal, Devisinghpura, the Jal Mahal, and Kanwar Nagar — before I caught my evening flight to Mumbai and onward to the Bay Area.
<br/>
<br/>
A truly magical weekend to celebrate a legend, and one of the most memorable India trips I've had.
