As my first official post, this is more breadth than depth — no single thesis, and not much analysis. That feels reasonable while I figure out what I actually like writing about. Split into Tech and Books, here are a few things I'm thankful for this year and why; each came into existence (or into my life) this year.

Before I split categorically, above all I'm thankful for the family, friends, and good health I've been fortunate to have this year. Not everyone has that fortune, so it deserves its gratitude.

Now, into the categories:

<h2 id="heading1">TECH</h2>
<h3 id="heading2"><a href = "https://www.headspace.com/" target="_blank">Headspace</a></h3>

I'm no Dalai Lama (or <a href = "https://www.cnbc.com/2018/03/16/bridgewater-associates-ray-dalio-meditation-is-key-to-my-success.html" target="_blank"> Ray Dalio</a>), but Headspace is a solid on-ramp to meditation. Built on a freemium model, it starts newcomers with a "Basics" series of 10 sessions with escalating expectations. My favorite lesson: think of your mind as a mid-summer sky, with clouds as the thoughts passing by. The goal isn't to stop thoughts, but to watch them pass rather than travel with them.


<h3 id="heading2"><a href = "https://art19.com/shows/pivot-with-kara-swisher-and-scott-galloway" target="_blank">Pivot</a></h3>
A recent discovery, but a fantastic weekly listen from
<a href = "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Swisher" target="_blank"> Kara Swisher</a> and
<a href = "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Galloway_(professor)" target="_blank">Scott Galloway</a>. As much as I appreciate the tech industry, it needs to be held accountable, and they do a great job of discussing its wins and losses. They can be harsh on companies, but it's worth a listen whether you work in tech or not.


<h3 id="heading2"><a href = "https://www.headspace.com/" target="_blank">Wrists Matter</a></h3>
Over half of jobs now require tech skills to operate a computer, a number expected to top 75% in the next five years, so healthy typing matters. Before 2017 I was careless with my ergonomics, contorting my wrists to use a laptop on a high table or in bed. Then the pain and tingling arrived. With stretching from a physical therapist and two ergonomic keyboards (and two mice) — one for home, one for work — the pain receded significantly. They run $200–500, but if forearm pain is hampering your work, invest in an ergonomic keyboard, mouse, chair, even a medical assessment (googling "ergonomic keyboard" is a fine start). Computer use isn't going anywhere.

<h2 id="heading1">BOOKS</h2>
<h3 id="heading2"><a href = "https://www.amazon.com/Predictably-Irrational-Revised-Expanded-Decisions/dp/0061353248" target="_blank">Predictably Irrational</a></h3>
A 2008 book by current Duke and ex-MIT behavioral economics professor Dan Ariely. It refutes classical economics' assumption that humans are rational, going a step further to show we're irrational in systematic, modelable patterns — predictably irrational. Heavy on logic and case studies, and made more interesting by the painful experience in Ariely's late teens that shaped his thinking.

<h3 id="heading2"><a href = "https://www.amazon.com/Hillbilly-Elegy-Memoir-Family-Culture/dp/0062300547" target="_blank">Hillbilly Elegy</a></h3>
J.D. Vance's vivid memoir of present-day life in the <a href="https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Rust_Belt">Rust Belt</a>. It leans conservative, but it lets the reader empathize with the Central and Southern U.S. Whatever your politics, it's a useful lens into the ideology of much of the population that voted for Trump in 2016 — a reminder of the deep cultural divides we need to heal. A good follow-up is "<a href = "https://www.amazon.com/Between-World-Me-Ta-Nehisi-Coates/dp/0451482212" target="_blank">Between the World and Me</a>" by Ta-Nehisi Coates.

<h3 id="heading2"><a href = "https://www.amazon.com/Sapiens-Humankind-Yuval-Noah-Harari/dp/0062316095" target="_blank">Sapiens</a></h3>
Yuval Harari splits human history into revolutions to challenge the common assumption that happiness tracks societal progress. It adds perspective on the purpose and sources of happiness and contentment in your life.

<h3 id="heading2">Graduation</h3>
Mixed feelings about Princeton. While I am grateful for my time there, I don't miss the institution/academics nearly as much as the people. After college, it's harder to be lackadaisical about preserving the strength of your social circles. If your friends are spread between 4-5 cities, you can't just bang on their door one morning, wake them up, and go out for brunch. Being spread out from college friends makes you more appreciative of your past experiences with them. It keeps you grounded in that it makes you not take your social experiences for granted, and forces you to timebox periods when you can catch up with the people you care about.
