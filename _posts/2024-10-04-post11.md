<br/>
If a U.S. or Canada resident wants an international trip in six days, working most of the time while avoiding nearly all jetlag, Mexico City is the place.
<br/>
<br/>
N and I went to thoroughly experience the city and visit Teotihuacan. After S1's 30th birthday on the evening of 09/29, S2 kindly woke early Sunday, came from Fremont to San Jose, and dropped me at the airport at 8:15am. G had driven N over, so we rendezvoused and boarded for LA. After a 48-minute layover at LAX, we flew to CDMX and landed early. With baggage storage saturated, N and I had to check our carry-ons — his was among the first five bags out, mine among the last five. With the Kahlo museum closing at 6pm, we tabled it for another day, dropped our things at the Airbnb, and headed to dinner in Roma Norte. En route we had a fun chat with the Uber driver about pop and rock — Bruno Mars, Metallica, Harry Styles, Cardi B, and their differences. Then ~6 tacos each, a long stroll through Parque México with live music, and a 40-minute walk each way to CHU-CHO, the highest-rated churrería in a two-mile radius, made for an exquisite evening. If you've never had amaranth, cinnamon, and dark chocolate fudge on a churro, add it to the list.
<br/>
<br/>
Since I was traveling on a work week, I woke early Monday. N grabbed pastries from the panadería, and we saved our appetite for the evening's all-Spanish Taco Tour. After a full day of work, we met at Taqueria Orinoco at 5pm for tacos de trompo (pork). Our guide Nene explained that trompos are the popular tops spun with a wound string, and that the name and method derive from lamb shawarma introduced by Lebanese migrants in the early 20th century (gyros and shawarma look much like a trompo). Mother Nature then made fools of us: having cleverly left both umbrellas at the Airbnb, we alternated sharing Nene's umbrella in the torrential downpour and taking our unplanned second showers of the day. After al pastor at Tacos de Guisado Lucho, savory fish tacos at Tres Galeones, and vegan tacos at La Pitahaya (hongos y coliflor), we ended with churros at Churreria El Moro. We said goodbye to Nene, N signed their OAI offer an hour later, and we celebrated with drinks at El Palenquito.
<br/>
<br/>
Tuesday's workday started early, and around 1:30pm we made our way to Pujol for a 2pm reservation. Pujol earned two Michelin stars in 2024 and is currently the country's highest-rated restaurant, tied with Quintonil. Since 2pm on 10/01 was the only slot that worked, I brought my work laptop along. The meal took over three hours and was the greatest dining experience of our lives: smoked baby corn elote, bluefin tuna & abalone with chile pasado, striped bass with green pistachio mole, medium-rare wagyu with chile ajo and roasted eggplant, mole madre & mole nuevo, and mango sake sorbet with fermented lime granité and yuzu. Every dish drew out flavors and emotions we hadn't felt from food before. Afterward we hit the Zócalo at night — numerous lighted displays of figures from Mexican independence and revolutionary history — followed by dinner at Azul Histórico. Zócalo was the Aztecs' ceremonial center at Tenochtitlan, and it was incredible to see figures like Miguel Hidalgo, Emiliano Zapata, Benito Juárez, and Quetzalcoatl in the plaza.
<br/>
<br/>
Wednesday, our first day leaving the city, we woke at 7am for the bus to Teotihuacan. Our guide Hermes was joyful, hilarious, and full of knowledge, relearning us on the city's cosmopolitan nature, its efficient compound housing, and its layered culture. Murals of deities sat alongside persistent animal and human sacrifice, and economically, black obsidian workshops around the Calle de los Muertos sustained trade networks for jade, feathers, and cacao. After the tour, we grabbed local conchas, spent a few hours at the Anthropology Museum in Chapultepec Park absorbing Aztec, Oaxacan, and Mayan history, and stopped at an Indian-Mexican fusion spot for dinner.
<br/>
<br/>
Thursday morning we packed quickly and caught our flight in good time. Overall, an amazing trip — incredible food and ample chances to speak with locals.
