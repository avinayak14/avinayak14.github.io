<br/>
KD asked S and me to plan his bachelor party in early 2025. After brainstorming a potpourri of U.S. cities, we settled on Kansas City for May 16–18 — central, and a place he'd never visited. One caveat: since K is the definition of chill/vibes/spontaneity, we decided in his honor to reveal neither the weekend nor the location until we set foot on the arriving flight, aided by tons of planning on the side with A. He thought he had a spa weekend plus a shot at a Bay to Breakers PR. Alas, it wasn't so :)
<br/>
<br/>
Thursday the 15th I crashed at S's place, and Friday morning we aimed to surprise K first thing. Our Uber reached their place at 6:45; we rang the doorbell to wake him and blasted Danza Kuduro over a speaker — a memory I'll never forget, with A capturing it all on video.
<br/>
<br/>
We had a three-hour layover in Burbank where we pretended we were headed to LA, grabbing tasty breakfast sandwiches at Porto's. After a quick Crumbl stop, it was time to head back. At the gate, we finally revealed we were going to Kansas City — he was palpably surprised, and excited. After sending plenty of pics of the unbridled quantities of Taylor merch we saw at the airport to the group chats, we made our way to the Airbnb to meet all the guys. For dinner, BBQ had to come first, so we hit the famed Jack Stack Barbecue in the Crossroads Arts district — it supplanted Panther City in Fort Worth as the best I've ever had. Brisket, burnt ends, pulled pork, bacon mac-n-cheese, cheddar jalapeño cornbread, and onion rings; and we felt like we'd earned it after taking the long way around the block (0.4 miles) exiting the Grunauer beer garden, instead of turning right and walking 50 feet.
<br/>
<br/>
The next morning we hit Cafecito Lindo, a Mexican breakfast café in Lenexa, its tortas nearly on par with the ones N and I had in Mexico City last year. Our plan was to ride the KC Streetcar (free transit, highly endorsed by K) to local landmarks: Union Station (with miniature train exhibits), the National WWI Museum and Memorial (my first WWI-exclusive museum), the Crown Center, and River Market (where we grabbed board games like Coup and One Night Werewolf). A fun variety of lore that day, from WWI's artillery/air/flight/submarine strategies to the 1856 sinking of the Steamboat Arabia. To cap the evening, Blue Sushi Sake and Grill — crab rangoons, yellowtail, dozens of rolls, and sake — before chilling at the Airbnb and playing all the board games we'd bought. A's bachelorette party may have out-drank us fivefold; they're bigger legends than us.
<br/>
<br/>
With most of us flying out Sunday afternoon/evening, we grabbed mochi donuts at the famous Blackhole Bakery first. It's an assembly-line spot where food is ready before you reach checkout; we changed our minds so many times it may have been the first time food waited on customers rather than the reverse. The donuts were excellent. Then S and K dropped me at the airport to catch my flight to DC.
<br/>
<br/>
Overall, a successful trip to two new states (KS & MO) celebrating one of my favorite people!
