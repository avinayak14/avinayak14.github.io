<br/>
06/13–06/14 was another absolute blockbuster of a weekend, Thursday through Sunday, celebrating two (more) of my favorite people on the planet.
<br/>
<br/>
S/S (plus A/H + A/S) and I were on the same Thursday morning flight SFO–IAD, so we met at the gate, where they'd saved me a breakfast sandwich that held me over through all our rising hunger. The flight was uneventful, and I caught about 3.5 hours of z's, useful frontloading for the weekend. S and I were K's designated chauffeurs, which meant waking around 7:45am Friday and 6:15am Saturday (still about three hours after A's wake-up on wedding day). After landing at IAD, the three of us picked up the rental car — AJ had already reached the hotel — then grabbed Chipotle before reaching the lobby around 9:45pm for food and much-needed TBC catch-up banter.
<br/>
<br/>
The next morning S and I woke and grabbed breakfast before shuttling KD to the Grah Shanti/Haldi. After morning pujas and food, and smearing KDAP with haldi, we procured 3–4 massive jugs of freezing water to dump on them by surprise. After a lunch of kachoris, pooris, and sabzis, we dropped KD at the hotel to rest and change. After a 1.5-hour nap, we shuttled K to Red Rock Wilderness for the pre-sangeet photoshoot, arriving around the same time as E+Z driving A over, catching some STUNNING couple shots. Around 6:30 we left for the Garba+Sangeet+Mehndi — an incredible night of food, dancing, and festivity (the corn chaat, real-time pani puri with pineapple and jaljeera, and the rest were S-tier). I got mehndi around the same time as G, and after plenty of dancing, capped the night with delicious jalebi and rabdi. Phenomenal — but after dropping K back around midnight, the job was only half done :)
<br/>
<br/>
Saturday we woke around 6:15am (SS a bit before me) for a 6:45am departure, pushed slightly as K did his photoshoot at the hotel. We transported him by around 7:50am, and I made the unfortunate mistake of bringing him coffee and leaving it at the hotel. After the bridesmaids' and groomsmen's photoshoots between 8:45 and 9am, K's side congregated near White's Ferry for the Baraat (great to meet AP + ST too). The baraat was a resounding success — a large collection of Punjabi/Bollywood/Western bangers, capped with a melodious Sri Lankan song. The ceremony was a beautiful amalgam of Gujarati and Sri Lankan rites (Agni Pooja, Daegili Baendima, Bulath Piligeneema, and Vidaai). I got emotional only twice, once when A walked out to "Har Ghadi" as the ceremony started (the other was K slow-dancing with his mom to "Sweet Child O' Mine" at the reception). We tried to save K's shoes, but A's buff cousins didn't spare us a chance. After the ceremony, a wonderful lunch of roasted and fried long bhindi, jalfrezi, paneer, and more. Before breaking, there was a coin game where A demolished K by finding a ring faster in a bucket of coins and milk. Should've warmed him up at the bachelor party!
<br/>
<br/>
S and I were DEEPLY, and I mean DEEPLY, hoping for a nap, but time was unrelenting. We managed about 20 minutes at the hotel to freshen up and change before heading back to White's Ferry for the reception. The chicken 65, gobi manchurian, and aam palak chaat were by far some of my favorite dishes ever, and each table had an event from KDAP's love story written as a cookbook-style recipe. I really wonder who's responsible for making me a foodie in the past five years.
<br/>
<br/>
The speeches and dances crept up faster than expected, but all went well. AJ, SS, and I gave a group speech of two-minute vignettes each, well-received. Glad (read: relieved) my impression landed; it was a bit nervewracking. Then the choreo sequence, our post-college friends' number (to Mauja Hi Mauja) closing the performance, and three-plus hours on the dance floor with breaks for It's-Its, gulab jamun cheesecakes, and cheese masala fries. S and I were dead exhausted by the end, but made the afterparty for a bit and returned the rental car the next morning before our flight.
<br/>
<br/>
Like the Doshansari weekend, the Karvokhi weekend was truly magical — we killed that shit, ngl — and I'm constantly reminded that the people we celebrated made it as magical as the festivities, food, and alcohol (though those definitely assisted).
