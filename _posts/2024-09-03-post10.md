<br/>
A trip to Japan was long overdue, and I'm glad I squeezed one in before starting the new gig.
<br/>
<br/>
On Aug 25, K+A picked me up around 10:30am and we played pickleball in Fremont with S. On the way to Sliver Pizzeria for a post-cardio pizza extravaganza, we stopped at a light at Walnut and Paseo Padre, where an 80-year-old woman with a walker tripped and fell on her left cheekbone. We rushed out, helped her indoors, and called paramedics (though she said she didn't need them). Once we'd escorted her into Kaiser for support, we continued to lunch, joined by S+S. Afterward K+A dropped me at SFO, and after an abnormally fast security line I caught my evening flight, landing at Narita at JST nighttime on the 26th. The Suica card purchase was critical.
<br/>
<br/>
At 5:50am sharp I hit the hotel onsen — fantastic. Ten minutes in the hot springs, five in the sauna, then ten more in the springs. After showering and a long walk around Akasaka, my friend L and I spent a few hours in Ginza before I left for Kyoto. Nearly every store was closed until 11am, but we browsed Zara (better selection than SF or NYC) and several malls. Around 11 I checked out and took the Shinkansen from Tokyo Station to Kyoto. Despite hitting ground speeds I've never been near (~186 mph), it felt remarkably smooth. The speed made low-altitude clouds move with the green hills at astonishing celerity — best captured in the video I took from the window. In Kyoto I dropped my bags and went straight to Osaka to meet my college friend M. We found an excellent gyukatsu spot with fresh Wagyu and a yakiniku setup, cooking our own meat over fire-heated wood charcoal, KBBQ-style. Then we walked Dotonbori (Osaka's symbolic business and shopping district), bought streetwear from mom-and-pop shops and Uniqlo, and hit Don Quijote (the name swaps the "x" from Cervantes' novel for a "j," since J stands for Japan), Japan's largest discount store. There may or may not have been gratuitous purchases of edible matcha paraphernalia. We then headed back to the station — M and her dad needed to return to Kobe for the night — and I departed for Kyoto.
<br/>
<br/>
Aug 28 was as jam-packed as the 27th. After a morning pastry and mocha, I met friends S and M (from Miami) at Karasume Oike and we headed to the Fushimi Inari Taisha shrine. It can't be described easily: around 10K torii gates, the most famous path being the Senbon Torii, and a multitude of fox (kitsune) statues, some holding keys or jewels. I've never seen such a juxtaposition of action and peace — the lower shrine alive with ceremonies and tourists, the higher steps quieting to nothing but water and birds. The torii look identical top to bottom, yet read completely differently in their surroundings. We ended with shaved ice in Fukakusayabunouchicho, then visited Gion, Pontocho Park, and Yasaka Shrine. By 3:30pm hangriness overtook all else, and we found delicious pork yaki gyoza near Sakuranocho. Then S & M somehow convinced me into a fish spa with red garra that nibble the dead skin off your feet — uncomfortable for ten straight minutes, but we left with a slight pep in our step. After tours of the Six Senses and Four Seasons Kyoto, we called it a night.
<br/>
<br/>
Aug 29 was jam-packed with rain and landmark navigation. After a quick NHK TV station drop-by, the Kyoto International Manga Museum traced a once-local, now-global art form through the Edo, Meiji, Taisho, Showa, and Heisei periods. The mixing of art forms has fascinated me for decades (acting and music within musical theater), and the recent blend of isekai fantasy with gourmet manga is an intriguing bridge between reality and fantasy. I'm a moderate manga consumer at best, but the history and technique were fascinating. After a dry blanket massage at Makapuu and ramen and sake at a local eatery, I capped the day at Kinkaku-ji (the Golden Pavilion) and the Arashiyama Bamboo Garden, with seared salmon sushi and a canelé omakase à la mode.
<br/>
<br/>
Immediately after my brain had spent three days unconsciously praising Japan's transit, I reached Kyoto Station Friday morning to find all Shinkansen to Tokyo via Nagoya cancelled due to Typhoon Shanshan. After nearly an hour in the customer-support line with a hundred other tourists (whose lack of Japanese slowed ticket processing considerably), I found an alternate route via Tsuruga — eight hours instead of three. The only other option, a flight from Osaka, also had a non-negligible cancellation risk, so I chose Tsuruga. The conductor said parties of two or more would stand the whole way; traveling alone, I'd only have to stand on the Tsuruga-to-Tokyo leg. Through some expedited-line luck, I schemed my way into a seat on both trains and reached Tokyo by evening, after about seven hours.
<br/>
<br/>
Saturday morning, nostalgia carried me to the Harry Potter café, then Tokyo Tower (officially the Japan Radio Tower, Nippon denpato). Its lattice resembles the Eiffel Tower, serving as both a communications tower and observation deck for 65+ years. The Top Deck (819 ft) far exceeded the Main Deck (490 ft), and one quirk is that indoors the universal greeting is "Wio!" with raised index, middle, and ring fingers. Contrary to popular belief, "WIO" doesn't mean "tower" — it's the shape of the radio-tower map symbol (a W, a descending line as the I, and a circle at the base as the O). After pong at the Minatoku Sports Complex (beating a prefecture team member!) and lunchtime yakitori, my SF friend J — staying with a host family for a few weeks — met me in Shinagawa. We grabbed soft serve and boba, tried an Oolong tea cocktail and a Yamazaki highball at an Italian restaurant, and I finished the night with a nice onsen. I'd hoped to see S, A, and Z, but they had tight schedules with train delays and had to head to the airport; I'll catch them in the Bay.
<br/>
<br/>
After checking out on 9/1, I saw a few more gardens, had brunch, hung with J at the Harry Potter gift shop in Minato, and bought gifts before my evening flight back to SFO. A phenomenal trip — the travel bug (not COVID, the metaphorical one) has definitely bitten.
